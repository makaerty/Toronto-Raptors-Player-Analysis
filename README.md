# Toronto Raptors | Player Performance Analysis

## Project Overview

This project evaluates Toronto Raptors player performance during the 2025–26 NBA season using Microsoft Excel.

The primary objective is to identify individual strengths, weaknesses, and potential development opportunities by comparing players against NBA positional benchmarks.

## Objectives

- Evaluate the performance of the Raptors' selected starting five and sixth man.
- Compare individual player statistics against NBA averages for their respective positions.
- Identify areas of competitive advantage and potential improvement.
- Translate statistical findings into basketball-related recommendations.

## Methodology

**1. Data preparation:** Organized NBA player statistics by position and filtered the dataset to include players who appeared in more than 41 games.

**2. Statistical analysis:** Selected performance metrics relevant to each position, including scoring, shooting efficiency, playmaking, rebounding, and defensive contributions.

**3. Benchmarking:** Compared player performance with NBA positional averages.

**4. Visualization:** Used Excel charts to highlight statistical differences.

**5. Evaluation:** Assessed individual strengths, weaknesses, and potential development priorities.

**6. Bench Player Benchmarking**

For the sixth-man analysis, the San Antonio Spurs' bench statistics from the 2025–26 NBA season were used as the benchmark instead of overall NBA positional averages.

The Spurs' bench was selected as a reference for high-performing second-unit production, based on its identification as the NBA's best bench in this analysis.

This approach allowed for a comparison of Jamal Shead's performance against a strong bench unit, highlighting his contributions and potential areas for improvement.

## Tools Used

- Microsoft Excel
- Statistical benchmarking
- Data visualization
- Basketball performance analysis

## Key Findings

### 1. Immanuel Quickley — Efficient Playmaking
- Averaged **5.9 assists and 1.5 turnovers**, producing an AST/TOV ratio of **3.93**, compared with the PG benchmark of 2.53.
- Scored **16.4 PPG**, exceeding the positional average of 12.2.
- Demonstrated strong ball control and above-average scoring production.

### 2. RJ Barrett — Strong Scoring, Inconsistent Three-Point Shooting
- Averaged **19.3 PPG**, significantly above the SG benchmark of 9.8.
- Recorded **55.0% eFG%**, exceeding the positional average of 53.7%.
- Three-point shooting (**33.9%**) remained below the positional benchmark of 36.3%, indicating an opportunity for improvement.

### 3. Brandon Ingram — Primary Offensive Contributor
- Averaged **21.5 PPG**, compared with the SF benchmark of 11.7.
- Demonstrated strong playmaking and rebounding contributions, with **3.7 assists and 5.6 rebounds** per game.
- Recorded **38.2% three-point shooting**, above the positional average of 35.9%.

### 4. Scottie Barnes — Versatile Two-Way Player
- Exceeded positional averages in scoring, rebounding, assists, steals, and blocks.
- Averaged **5.9 assists**, compared with the PF benchmark of 1.9, highlighting exceptional playmaking production for his position.
- Turnovers (**2.6 vs. 1.8**) represent a potential improvement area.

### 5. Jakob Poeltl — Efficient Interior Scoring
- Recorded **70.0% eFG%**, substantially exceeding the center benchmark of 58.0%.
- Averaged **7.0 rebounds and 2.5 offensive rebounds**, both above positional averages.
- Free-throw shooting (**60% vs. 72%**) and blocks (**0.7 vs. 0.9**) were below their respective benchmarks.

### 6. Jamal Shead — Strong Bench Playmaking
- Averaged **5.4 assists**, significantly exceeding the bench benchmark of 1.76.
- Recorded an AST/TOV ratio of **3.86**, compared with the bench benchmark of 2.10.
- Shooting efficiency remained an improvement area, with **45.0% eFG%** compared with the bench average of 56.4%.

## Overall Conclusion

The analysis highlights several strengths within the Toronto Raptors' selected rotation, particularly **playmaking, individual scoring production, and interior scoring efficiency**.

The most notable development opportunities include **three-point shooting consistency, free-throw efficiency, turnover management, and bench scoring efficiency**.

These findings can support player development priorities and future roster evaluation decisions.
